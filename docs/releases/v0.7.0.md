Fretforge 0.7.0 is about playing along: your live rig and the song now share one engine, a coach listens to what you play, takes keep your dry guitar for re-amping, and gear you capture yourself shows up as named blocks.

## Download

`Fretforge-0.7.0-x86_64.AppImage` (Linux x86-64, tested target Nobara 44), with a `.sha256` checksum.

```sh
chmod +x Fretforge-0.7.0-x86_64.AppImage && ./Fretforge-0.7.0-x86_64.AppImage
```

Docker: `ghcr.io/goglesquirmintontheiii/fretforge:v0.7.0` (amd64 and arm64).

## Highlights

- **One audio clock for the song, stems and rig.** While the live rig is on, the song plays from the same engine as your guitar: inside the JACK engine in the desktop app, or on the same audio context in the browser. They stay aligned through a whole song, and speed, loops and pitch shift change only the song.
- **Main and headphone mixes.** Song and rig levels for the main output, plus a second mix on the desktop engine's `phones_L`/`phones_R` ports (routable in the rig's Audio and MIDI panel).
- **Per-song live rig.** Pick a rig for each song and switch rigs or snapshots at chosen bars or at every section marker.
- **Gig mode.** Play a playlist as a setlist: each song brings its rig and backing track, Page Down / Page Up (what most page-turner and MIDI foot pedals send) move between songs, and the next song loads when one ends.
- **Practice coach.** Pitch and onset detection on your dry guitar, compared with the tab: right note, wrong note or missed, with your timing. Wait mode stops at each note until you play it, and every run is saved to the song's history.
- **Takes and re-amping.** Record the dry DI, the rig and the song together; re-amp the DI through any rig later; export parts as WAV or a mix with the backing track as MP3 or WAV. Takes are private.
- **Tone match.** Ranks your amp captures and cabs by how close they sound to a song's guitar stem and fits a parametric EQ to get closer. Save the result as a rig or use it for the song.
- **Automated capture training.** Record your guitar until you press stop, wait a few seconds, and Fretforge plays the recording out to your amp or pedal and records what comes back through the same input, lines the two up and trains a NAM model.
- **Named gear.** Give captures and IRs a device name and a setting. Each device becomes one block in the blocks list with a switch between its settings, for pedals, amps and cabs alike. TONE3000 tones with several models arrive grouped the same way.
- **One-click yt-dlp.** If yt-dlp is missing, a button downloads the official release and checks it against its published checksum.

## Fixes

- Progress messages from NAM training reach the app again (they went to the error stream during training).
- NAM training works on Python builds without Tk (headless Linux, some Homebrew builds).
- The player's side tabs scroll when they don't fit instead of squeezing, and a text tab's chord panel starts closed on phones.

## Known limits

- The coach judges single notes by pitch; chords are judged by timing and one matching note. Fast legato and heavy distortion before the coach's input confuse it, which is why it listens before the rig.
- Tone match compares spectra only. Gain, feel and dynamics are up to your ears, and playing your own DI of the song gives a fairer comparison than the built-in riff. It compares up to 16 amp captures (named gear first) and 24 IRs.
- The separate headphone mix needs the desktop app's low-latency engine; in the browser there is one mix.
- Automated capture records through the browser's audio input; the wait before playback can be set from 2 to 30 seconds, or until you press Continue. Training on a CPU takes a long time.
- The capture alignment and NAM training were tested end to end with a simulated amp (distortion plus latency) and NAM 0.13; the JACK playback, recording, headphone mix and the capture flow were tested against stand-ins, not with a real interface and amp.
